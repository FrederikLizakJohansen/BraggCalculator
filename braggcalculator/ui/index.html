A complete scientific workflow, not a fit button

In plain languageA powder pattern is a compressed fingerprint. Several structures may reproduce it. This application keeps the candidate, experiment, released parameters and every continuation together so the conclusion can be audited.
TheoryThe calculated profile maps a periodic density to complex structure factors, intensities, powder peaks and an instrument-broadened curve. Each step loses information, so agreement at the final curve does not prove structural uniqueness.
What to look forFollow the tabs from left to right. Demand a small, unstructured residual; a measurable separation between candidates; and identifiable parameters. If any gate fails, report the ambiguity rather than forcing a winner.

Observed and calculated powder profiles

In plain languageThe black curve is the measurement; the colored curve is what the selected structure predicts after refinement. Good overlap means the model reproduces the pattern, but it does not by itself prove the structure is unique.
TheoryThe profile is μ(2θ)=B(2θ)+Σₕ Aₕ φ(2θ−2θₕ). Rwp is the weighted root-mean-square residual normalized by the observed intensity.
What to look forCheck every angle, not only the largest peaks. Compare candidates under identical policy. Watch the optimization trace: a flat trace with a high residual suggests a model/profile limitation; a still-falling trace may justify continuation.

Standardized residual and informative regions

In plain languageThis plot asks where the prediction misses by more than the measurement uncertainty. A value of +3 means the observed signal is about three stated standard deviations above the calculation.
TheoryFor independent errors, dᵢ=(yᵢ−μᵢ)/σᵢ. The largest |dᵢ| regions identify local lack of fit; correlated errors require whitening rather than pointwise division.
What to look forRandom scatter around zero is healthier than long runs of one sign. Repeated peak-shaped residuals indicate missing/shifted intensity. Broad waves suggest background or profile error. Do not release atoms merely to absorb an instrumental defect.

Candidate structure comparison

In plain languageThese are synchronized fractional a–b projections of the uploaded candidates. They show which atoms or motifs are actually different before asking whether powder diffraction can see that difference.
TheoryThe relationship classifier first removes representational choices and assigns compatible (I), commensurate (II), or unrelated (III) lattices. Reflection-wise phase comparison is only valid after a reciprocal mapping exists.
What to look forCheck formula, cell metrics, species mapping and displaced sites. If the UI says regime III, do not interpret hkl-wise phase differences; use profiles and pair distributions instead.

Complex mismatch: amplitude versus phase

In plain languageEach dot is one matched reflection. Horizontal displacement means different predicted strength; vertical displacement means different calculated phase. The center means agreement and the circle edge means severe disagreement.
TheoryThe bounded coordinates obey r²=|Fᴮ−Fᴬ|²/(|Fᴬ|+|Fᴮ|+ε)²≤1 after origin alignment. D²SF=D²amp+D²phase. Experimental powder intensities contain |F|², not phase, so vertical differences may be hidden.
What to look forA vertical cloud with a similar powder fit is a phase-loss signature. Horizontal spread affects ideal intensities. This is a model-to-model diagnostic—calculated phase is not claimed to have been measured.

Resolution-defined peak groups and overlap

In plain languageOne visible powder peak can contain many reflections. The bars rank the strongest groups; the effective-reflection count says how mixed each feature is.
TheoryReflections separated by less than a declared FWHM in Q are grouped. Nₑff=exp(−Σpₕ log pₕ), where pₕ is each reflection's intensity fraction. Nₑff≈1 is clean; larger values mean stronger overlap.
What to look forPrefer discriminating groups with large intensity and low Nₑff. A candidate-specific reflection buried beneath a common strong peak may exist theoretically but be unusable experimentally.

Parameter sensitivity, rank and correlation

In plain languageSensitivity asks whether a parameter changes the pattern. Correlation asks whether two parameters make almost the same change. A fit cannot separately determine indistinguishable directions, however good its Rwp.
TheoryJᵢⱼ=∂μᵢ/∂pⱼ and H=JᵀWJ. Sensitivity is ||Jⱼ||W. Singular values determine data rank; covariance is only locally meaningful when supported directions are sufficiently independent.
What to look forLow bars mean little experimental leverage. Red/blue heatmap cells near |ρ|=1 mean unsafe joint release. Compare data rank with parameter count; prior-restored rank is chemical information, not new diffraction information.

Refined values, constraints and release state

In plain languageThis is the scientific ledger: what was allowed to move, what stayed fixed, and what values resulted. A parameter value without its release policy is not a reproducible refinement result.
TheoryPositive quantities use constrained transforms; occupancies use simplexes; anisotropic U uses a positive-definite parameterization; lattice modes obey the loaded crystal symmetry unless explicitly broken.
What to look forCheck boundary-like occupancies or displacement factors, implausible cell changes and large coordinate shifts. A restraint-stabilized value must be described as conditional on that restraint.

Where and how to measure next

In plain languageIf today's experiment cannot separate the models, this tab estimates whether better resolution, more Q range, or different scattering contrast would help.
TheoryExpected separation is Σᵢ(μAᵢ−μBᵢ)²/σ²ᵢ under the stated count/background model. Larger values mean greater predicted discrimination, conditional on the forward and uncertainty assumptions.
What to look forCompare relative rankings, then inspect the most informative Q. These are design predictions, not guaranteed outcomes. Real instrument backgrounds, absorption and sample effects may change the ranking.

Run lineage, assumptions and portable exports

In plain languageThis tab answers “what exactly produced this conclusion?” It keeps source checksums, run continuations, parameter policy, warnings and downloadable artifacts together.
TheoryReproducibility requires immutable inputs and explicit state transitions. Resume restores exact raw parameters but starts fresh optimizer moments; it is recorded as a child trace rather than silently extending a run.
What to look forArchive project.json, result JSON, profiles CSV, refined CIFs and audit JSON. Verify the dataset checksum before publication. Never detach a refined CIF from the data, policy and warnings that produced it.
